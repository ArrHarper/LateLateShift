Breece's Pieces

Cass · Pick #7

C+ Roster Grade

Top-two QB and top-four WR rooms, with the worst RB room in the league by a wide margin.

Here's a sentence no other recap gets: this team's keepers were worth one pick. Combined. Breece Hall's market price collapsed so far below his 2nd-round cost that he graded as the league's only negative keeper, nearly erasing Lamar's +25. The draft that followed was genuinely good — patient, value-positive, anchored by a Justin Jefferson who's among the five most valuable starters anyone in this league rosters. It still wasn't enough to climb out: eight of ten managers walk into September with a stronger lineup.

Every recap in this league tells the same story — keepers gave, drafts took. This one runs backwards: the keeper ledger opened at +1, and the draft spent seventeen rounds patiently trying to earn it back. The patience was real and the board score is positive, but the picks that filled the starting lineup came in at −15 — the surplus is sitting on the bench. Jefferson is a genuine cornerstone and Lamar behind him is a real one-two, which is why this doesn't grade worse than it does. Two stars and a running back room that's a weekly emergency is an eighth-best lineup. The lesson is already priced in: next summer, keep the player the market loves, not the one you do.

Keepers: The Breece Tax

Keeper · Round 5

The good half of the ledger: a top-3 fantasy quarterback kept for a 5th-round cost. Locking elite QB production while the room pays 2nds and 3rds for the position is real, repeatable value.

Keeper · Round 2

Breece Hall RB · NYJ −24 picks of value

The league's only negative keeper. Hall cost a 2nd-round slot while the market had drifted to the 4th — loyalty to the 2024 version of the player, billed at 2025 prices. Every other manager's keeper page is a brag sheet; this one is a receipt.
