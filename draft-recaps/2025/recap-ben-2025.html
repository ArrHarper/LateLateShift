Herbert Fully Loaded

Ben · Pick #9

B+ Roster Grade

Second-best WR room in the league, paired with the only negative QB value and a bottom-two TE room.

The top of this roster is elite and fairly bought: Lamb, A.J. Brown, and two kept starters at real discounts form the league's best receiver room. Then the draft's second half happened, and it happened hard — six consecutive-ish rounds of deep sleepers taken 15 to 61 picks before the market wanted them, adding up to a −190 value total that laps the field. Here's the twist: it barely dented the lineup. CeeDee Lamb and Malik Nabers are good enough to carry a fifth-best starting lineup on their own.

There are two drafts in this recap. The first built the best receiver room in the league — Lamb, Brown, and Nabers is a weekly cheat code — around two workmanlike keepers, and it's why this lineup projects fifth despite everything that followed. The second spent six rounds paying breakout prices for players whose breakouts remain theoretical, and billed the roster −190, the worst tab in the league by a distance. Nearly all of it landed on the bench, which is the only reason the draft grade isn't a disaster — but the starting picks were negative too. If two of those sleepers hit, the room will call it vision; if none do, one injury turns a strong lineup into a waiver-wire season.

Keepers: Two Solid Locks

A-

Keeper · Round 4

Malik Nabers WR · NYG +22 picks of value

A top-10 player kept for a 4th-round cost. The discount is modest because the cost is high — but locking a first-round talent before pick 32 is exactly what the keeper rule is for.

Keeper · Round 6

Kyren Williams RB · LAR +28 picks of value

Year two of this keep: a top-25 back for a 6th-round cost, in one of the league's best offenses. The quiet, repeatable kind of keeper value.

Draft Picks: Paying January Prices in August

C

✓ Biggest Value

CeeDee Lamb WR · Round 1

The first ten rounds produced no discount at all — Lamb, four picks past his league price, is the best of them, and he is a first-rounder bought at roughly first-round cost. He is also about 24% of this roster's projected value, so the early message of this draft was concentration, not bargain-hunting. The steal came in round fifteen, long after this section stops looking.

✗ Biggest Reach

Rashod Bateman WR · Round 12

Taken 61 picks before his league price in Round 12 — a fourth option in Baltimore's offense, drafted where the market had starting receivers available. The steepest single overpay on a board full of them.
