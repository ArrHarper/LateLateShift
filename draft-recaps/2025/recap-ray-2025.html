Big Diggs and My Chubb

Ray · Pick #8

B Roster Grade

Top-two RB room and a top-three TE room, with receivers the only group below league average.

Second-best keepers, second-best draft value, and not one decision anyone could argue with. Chase Brown and McConkey arrived at a combined +153 of keeper discount, and the draft stacked seven more steals on top, including a tight end room acquired almost entirely below market. What all that shopping didn't buy is a single player who wins a week by himself — nine good starters, no great one — and that's the whole gap between this roster and the top of the board.

Every individual decision here was right — 2nd in keeper equity, 2nd in draft value, seven steals and one mild reach. The grade is lower than that résumé because the grade is the roster, and this roster's best starter projects behind seven other managers' best starters. Nine good players and no dominant one is a fine way to make the playoffs and a hard way to win a title. The bet that changes it: Jeanty and Taylor out-producing the bottom-third offenses they're trapped in. Either becomes a genuine league-winner and this team stops being deep and starts being scary — the talent is there for it, the neighborhoods aren't.

Keepers: The Double Heist

A

Keeper · Round 12

Ladd McConkey WR · LAC +86 picks of value

A top-30 player kept for a 12th-round cost — the third-biggest keeper discount in the league. McConkey is the Chargers' entire passing identity, acquired for what most teams spend on a backup tight end.

Keeper · Round 9

Chase Brown RB · CIN +67 picks of value

A top-25 player for a 9th-round cost. Cincinnati's every-down back in one of the league's most pass-happy, points-adjacent offenses — the fifth-best keep in the league, and somehow the lesser of this team's two.
